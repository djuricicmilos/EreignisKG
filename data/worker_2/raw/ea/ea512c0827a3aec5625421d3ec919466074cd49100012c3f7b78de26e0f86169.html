Being3: being as beyng (being). Nevertheless, even the characterization of being as not-a-being, as the “other” of beings, is only a negative and transitional stage on the way to a more “positive” postmetaphysical approach. Being in the third and most comprehensive sense is precisely the differentiation of background and foreground as such. It is the “temporalization” of a present in the ecstatic unity of timeliness, the emergence of something against the background of the nothing, the complication of the multidimensional background into the complicated unity of the foreground. Speaking of “being” in this third sense, the later Heidegger most consistently spells it with the obsolete and “archaic” eighteenth-century orthography used by Hegel and Hölderlin, Seyn (perhaps best rendered with the equally obsolete Early Modern English variant “beyng,” used interchangeably with “beynge,” “beinge,” and “being” in the sixteenth century), supposedly in order to indicate that it is the “origin” or productive source of being1, its presupposition, and in this sense “older” than the being known to metaphysics. In “On the Question of Being” (1955), Heidegger crosses out “being” (Sein) in order to emphasize the way in which this meaning-generating difference refuses to be conceptualized and designated in the manner of a determinate entity—and also, he tells us, in order to indicate by typographical means the way in which what is traditionally named by “being,” i.e., presence, points beyond itself toward the four background dimensions of the fourfold (Geviert; see Chapter 5; WM, 410–12/PM, 310–11). Seyn/beyng is simply a name for the postmetaphysical perspective on being into which the beingness traditionally pursued by metaphysics—presence as such in its various metaphysical configurations—is incorporated as a foreground aspect that is constantly differentiated from a background dimension of radical nonpresence. Ereignis, event, is accordingly a name for the specific postmetaphysical historical experience and articulation of beyng in terms of this dimensionality.

The “ontological difference” thus turns out to be a preliminary name for beyng (being3) itself insofar as the latter is approached from the framework of fundamental ontology, in which “being” generally means being1 and “sense of being” means being2. As the happening of ecstatic timeliness in its complicated unity, Dasein’s existence is precisely the process whereby beyng as ontological difference is continually carried out as the present is disclosed as a complicated unity of the temporal dimensions.
