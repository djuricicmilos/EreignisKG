Remarks I [10–11]

Beyng and “death”—Whatever “is” essential has its constancy in the inceptuality of the inceptual beginning of the coming. That it can come “again and again” and can be something futural. The possibility for this coming arises from a primordial having-been. Beyng must first return back to its past essential prevailing [Gewesung]. What has essentially prevailed [das Gewesende] as the origin of what is coming.

Being and Time is an initial attempt to comprehend this essence of beyng.5 This comprehension is impossible without the knowledge of Da-sein and the knowledge of how it essentially transforms everything and provides the first insights into the inner possibilities of being itself. The potentiality of being—“death.”

Yet because one anticipates nothing of the manner of questioning in Being and Time and nothing of the terrain opened up by such questioning, one reaches the curious conclusion that “all” there is to say about “death” has been said. While the work is nothing but the initial resonance of a meditation that experiences the truth of beyng in the terrain of Da-sein and not in the terrain of beings, nor of being ness (metaphysics).


Previously art as poetry grounded the festival. Nowadays we require “festivals,” i.e. mere fanfare, in order to “celebrate” “art,” which no longer exists. Where in all the world has there ever been a “Festival of Art”?6 This “idea” is the monstrous spawn of pure “technical understanding.”


The illusion that the past has nothing more to say to us is a result of straying into the incapacity to listen to the essential past.


Organization—they get upset because it is “empty.” There is no organization that is empty; for even when it seems to be empty, and at the same time is so necessary and appropriate just the way it is, then it seems empty because it is the organization of emptiness itself.

The organization of nihilism.
