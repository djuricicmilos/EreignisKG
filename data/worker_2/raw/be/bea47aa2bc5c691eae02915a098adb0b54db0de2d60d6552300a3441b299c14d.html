II. The Central Question of Western Metaphysics

“Inside”—memory still as the origin of the “self.” Therefore, the “there” is merely grasped in its spatiality, and this is misused for the sake of explanation; compare language!

Self-relation to the world as Dasein. Dasein is “spatial” insofar as it acknowledges “spaces” at all—that is, the fact that space opens and closes.

The vantage point of the central question need not be “from within” at all, insofar as we—the questioners—always belong to Being and are ourselves beings within it. But this “from within”—in the sense of one being among others—is it not a “from without,” and is this “within” itself not still viewed from the outside? In what sense? Because everything remains oriented toward what is already present, and one crouches “before” what is, thereby forgetting oneself or simply counting oneself among it, setting oneself apart, yet indeterminately and thus still counting as part of it—a purely numerical distinction. But what does it mean to set oneself apart—from where and to where? Precisely so that this very “among other things” becomes visible, is perceived, and is presented; what of this presentation—is it merely something countable and to be included in the count, or does it stand in a different relationship to being as such? What does Parmenides’s statement actually say! According to it, there is already more to this “from within” than mere belonging. This as such: that which perceives what it belongs to, and thus reveals itself as belonging to it and as perceiving (as the locus of truth). What does that mean? And how is this experience initially interpreted and applied?



10. The Guiding Question of “In the Midst” – “All Around”


It is only through the structure of the guiding question that the “in-the-middle” is foreshadowed, without the “in-the-middle” itself being able to be grounded by it; for the guiding question always takes Being, in one way or another, as “concrete,” “objective,” or—which amounts to the same thing—“subjective,” from the perspective of “consciousness.”

Initially: self-forgotten and therefore not objective either.
