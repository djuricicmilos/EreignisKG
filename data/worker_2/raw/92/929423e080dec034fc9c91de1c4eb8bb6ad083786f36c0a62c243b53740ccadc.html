Where does reflection on the structure originate? From the original posing of the fundamental question.



9. The Location of the Guiding Question


The location of the guiding question: the “inside” and “outside” / being-there. One asks from the “outside”—e.g., Nietzsche—and then “the world” is an incomprehensible problem. One asks from the “inside”—and then “the world” is the will to power. What do “inside” and “outside” mean here, and where does this distinction come from in the first place, and how does it enter into the guiding question, and what does it accomplish here?

1. The distinction is a “spatial” one. Inside and outside and the boundary? Thus, a question of space and “spatiality.” Cf. on time-space. Cf. Being and Time.

2. However, this distinction plays a special role in characterizing human beings and their relationship “to the world.” The soul “inside” and the “world” “outside” and the “boundary”? And what kind of “space” does the soul have, along with everything associated with it, e.g., sensation? From where is this even being viewed—human beings and their relationship itself again “from the outside” and thus “in relation to” an inside?

3. But what about the distinction with regard to being as a whole? —from the “inside,” i.e., from placing oneself within our inner self (will?!) in relation to the outside. The “opposite.”

4. The distinction thus always presupposes the self and, that is to say, the relationship to the world. This self-relationship to the world is and is more being-like than the rest of being, because it consists as an event of “being-there.”

This self-relationship to the world, however, is in itself earthly, because it is grounded—or rather, is not grounded—in “being-there,” and it forgets it and does not attain it. And in doing so, space is always “as such”—that is, within certain limits—and therefore the boundary between the “inside” and outside.
